Reading the source

Making the numbers agree

What the tutorial says

  • “Length: 25 inches”
  • “The height between these two points is 15 inches”
  • “Distance between the two points: 2.25 inches”
  • “Dimension: 1.5 inches”
  • “Two holes 0.5 inches, 3 inches apart”

Twenty-five inches and fifteen inches are both stated. They only make sense together.

  • 25 inches is the whole tyne, measured along the bar.
  • 15 inches is the straight part at the top.
  • The bend and the foot make up the remaining 10 inches.

The rake angle and the bend radius are our choice — the tutorial gives neither. We use 35 degrees and a 6 inch inner radius. Both are spreadsheet numbers, so change them if you disagree.

Part 2: Tine 0102 / 08

One sketch, the whole shape

The profile

Drawn on the YZ plane — the one SolidWorks calls Right.

  • A flat top edge, 2.25 inches wide, centred on the origin.
  • Two straight sides going down 15 inches.
  • Two arcs that share the same centre, so the bar keeps the same width all the way round the bend.
  • Two straight sides for the foot, and a flat end for the point.

Only one radius is dimensioned. The outer arc follows automatically, because the two arcs share a centre and the bar is 2.25 inches wide.

Part 2: Tine 0103 / 08

Constraints

Two rules people forget

Twenty rules pin this sketch down. These are the two that are easy to miss.

  • Parallel on the two sides of the foot. Without it the sketch still moves. Tangency alone does not hold an arc's end in place.
  • Symmetric about the origin point. Select the two top corners and the origin, press S, and the whole profile centres itself in one go.

At the four smooth joins use endpoint tangency only. Adding a coincident rule as well makes the two fight, and the sketch then produces no shape at all.

Part 2: Tine 0104 / 08

The trap from Deck 4, in practice

Dimension the reach, not the angle

We first tried an angle constraint for the rake. The solver flipped the foot backwards.

  • “35 degrees between these lines” is true of the tyne and its mirror image.
  • The sketch reported fully constrained and no errors. It was just the wrong shape.
  • Saying the point reaches 3.6 inches forward has only one answer.

The rake angle is still a named parameter. It drives the reach through a formula in the spreadsheet, so you can still type 30 degrees and get a 30 degree tyne.

Part 2: Tine 0105 / 08

Pads

From outline to solid

Two pads. The first makes the tyne, the second widens the top so it can be bolted down.

  1. Pad the profile, with Symmetric to plane ticked1.5 in
  2. New sketch on the XY plane
  3. Centred rectangle, 5 inches along the bar5 &times; 2.25 in
  4. Pad it downwards, with Reversed ticked1.25 in

Pad symmetrically so the tyne sits centred on whatever you position it with. Pad it one way and every placement in the assembly carries a correction you will forget.

Part 2: Tine 0106 / 08

The interface to Part 1

The mounting bolts

Two half-inch holes, 3 inches apart. The same 3 inches that is drilled in the frame.

  1. Sketch on the XY plane
  2. Circle at half the bolt spacing from the centre1.5 in
  3. Second circle made Symmetric about the Y axis, and Equal
  4. Pocket, through all

This is the joint that holds the machine together. It is worth checking against the frame before you go on — same diameter, same spacing.

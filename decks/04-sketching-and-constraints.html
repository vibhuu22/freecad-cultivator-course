The most important idea in this deck

What fully constrained means

A shape can move until you pin it down. Every rule you add takes away one way it could move.

  • A line can move four ways: each end can go left, right, up or down.
  • A circle can move three: its centre, and its size.
  • Add rules until nothing is left. FreeCAD then says fully constrained.

The frame outline has eight lines. That is thirty-two ways it could move, and thirty-two rules to hold it still.

Sketching and Constraints02 / 09

Do not leave it for later

Why it matters

An unfinished sketch will move on you. Usually at the worst possible moment.

  • The model stops surprising you. Change one dimension and only the thing you meant to change moves.
  • Your intention is written down. The sketch says “this rectangle is centred and 82 inches wide”, not “these lines happen to be here”.
  • It is the whole point. If the shape is not locked to the numbers, changing a number does nothing reliable.

All twenty sketches in this machine are fully constrained. It is a habit, and it takes seconds.

Relationships and numbers

The two kinds of constraint

Rules about relationships

RuleKeyIt says
CoincidentCthese two points are the same point
HorizontalHthis line is level
VerticalVthis line is upright
ParallelPthese two lines run the same way
TangentTthe curve meets the line smoothly
EqualEsame length, or same radius
SymmetricSthese mirror about that line

Rules with numbers

RuleKeyIt says
DistanceK Dthis is 15 inches long
Horizontal distanceLthese are 3 inches apart sideways
Vertical distanceIthese are 2 inches apart up and down
RadiusK Rthis arc is R6
DiameterK Othis hole is half an inch

Prefer the first kind. Two lines told to be equal stay equal forever. Two lines each measured 5 inches stay equal only until somebody edits one.

Worked example

A centred rectangle, step by step

The commonest shape in the whole machine. Watch the count on the right go down to nothing.

  1. Draw four lines corner to corner. FreeCAD adds the corner joins for you.8 left
  2. Horizontal on the top and bottom lines6 left
  3. Vertical on the left and right lines4 left
  4. Symmetric: two opposite corners about the origin. This centres it.2 left
  5. Horizontal distance on the bottom line: 82 in1 left
  6. Vertical distance on the side: 22 indone

One of the most useful rules there is

Symmetry beats copying

The frame needs matching bolt holes on both bars. Do not draw them twice.

  1. Draw and dimension the two holes on the front bar
  2. Draw two more circles roughly where the back pair goes
  3. Select a front centre, a back centre, then the X axis, and press SSymmetric
  4. Add Equal so the sizes follow as well

This is what SolidWorks calls Mirror Entities. It is not a copy. Move the front hole and the back one follows, always.

Sketching and Constraints06 / 09

Tangency

Trap one: joining a line to a curve

Where a straight line runs smoothly into an arc, you need endpoint tangency.

  • Click the end of the line and the end of the arc, then press T.
  • That one rule joins them and makes the join smooth.
  • Do not also add a coincident rule at the same corner.

If you add both, the two rules fight each other. FreeCAD says conflicting constraints, the sketch produces no shape at all, and the Pad that follows fails with a confusing message about a missing profile.

Sketching and Constraints07 / 09

Prefer distances to angles

Trap two: angles have two answers

“These two lines are 35 degrees apart” describes two different shapes. One leans forward, one leans back.

  • The solver picks whichever it finds first. It is not always the one you drew.
  • On the Tine 01 profile it flipped the whole foot backwards.
  • The fix: say how far forward the point reaches instead. That has only one answer.

It is also the better dimension. “How far forward does the point go?” is a question about the machine. “What is the angle at the bend?” is a question about the drawing.

Sketching and Constraints08 / 09

A routine that works

Sketching in the right order

Work this way

  1. Draw roughly. Do not chase exact positions with the mouse. Get the shape right.
  2. Add the relationship rules. Horizontal, vertical, tangent, equal, symmetric.
  3. Then add the numbers, watching the count go down.
  4. Stop at zero. Do not add one more for luck — that is the one that causes trouble.

Two habits worth forming

  • Name your dimensions. Double-click the constraint and call it FrameLength, not Constraint7. You need the name to drive it from a spreadsheet later.
  • Close your shapes. An open outline cannot be padded. If a Pad complains, look for a gap at a corner.

If a Pad or Pocket fails and the sketch looks perfectly fine, read the small message in the Sketcher panel first. It is usually telling you exactly what is wrong.

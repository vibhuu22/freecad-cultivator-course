Part 3: Tine 0201 / 07

Cross-check before you draw

Three numbers that check each other

The tutorial gives three dimensions for the same arc. That is a gift — you can check your reading before you draw.

  • “A radius of 16 inches on both sides”
  • “The height from this point to this point is 15 inches”
  • “From this point to the midpoint of the outer arc: 20.3 inches”

Take the radius and the height as given. The straight distance across the arc then works out at 19.7 inches. The tutorial says 20.3. Three per cent apart, so our reading is right.

Part 3: Tine 0202 / 07

Simpler than Tine 01

The profile

Two arcs sharing a centre, joined by four straight lines.

  • A flat top edge, 2.5 inches wide, centred on the origin.
  • Two short straight sides before the curve begins.
  • Two arcs sharing a centre, so the bar keeps the same width all the way down.
  • A flat end for the point.

The point has to sit square across the bar. Rather than an angle, tell FreeCAD that the arcs' shared centre point lies on the end line. A line through the centre of a circle is square to it by definition.

Reusing the interface

From outline to solid

Three of the four steps are exactly what you did on Tine 01.

  1. Pad the profile, symmetric1.25 in
  2. Sketch the head on the XY plane and pad it down5 &times; 2.5 in
  3. Two half-inch holes, 3 inches apart, pocketed throughsame as Tine 01
  4. Fillet the two corners of the pointR1

The bolt interface is deliberately identical. Either tyne fits any of the eighteen stations on the frame — which is how a real cultivator works, and it costs nothing to design in.

Part 3: Tine 0205 / 07

Fillet

Rounding the point

A 1 inch radius on a 2.5 inch bar makes the point very nearly a half-round.

  • A sharp corner on a wear part is where cracks start.
  • It is also where the blade bolts on, and a rounded seat spreads the load.
  • In soil a sharp corner wears round within an hour anyway.

Picking the two edges by eye is fine once. It is not repeatable — change something upstream and the edges get renumbered. Keep dress-up features to the end of the tree, and few.

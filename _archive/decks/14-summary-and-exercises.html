Summary and Exercises02 / 07

The whole course, condensed

Ten things worth remembering

  • Fully constrain every sketch. No exceptions.
  • Put sketches on origin planes, with offsets. Not on faces.
  • Use endpoint tangency where a line meets a curve, and nothing else at that corner.
  • Prefer distances to angles. An angle has two answers.
  • Read the message in the Sketcher panel. It tells you what is wrong.
  • A pocket cuts from its sketch plane onward. Put the plane where the cut starts.
  • You cannot pattern a pattern. Do the symmetry in the sketch.
  • Fillets and chamfers go last, and keep them few.
  • Put each part's origin on the face that matters — the one that bolts to something else.
  • Every dimension in a spreadsheet, named, with its unit.

Summary and Exercises04 / 07

Exercises 1 to 8

Exercises: get comfortable

Parameter changes

  1. Eleven tynes. Set the count to 11 and the frame to 101 inches. Report the new weight.
  2. A ten-foot machine. Decide the spacing first, then work out the count and the length.
  3. Re-rake Tine 01 to 25 and then 45 degrees. Which would you specify for hard ground, and why?
  4. Add blade bolt holes. You decide the spacing — and justify it.

Modelling jobs

  1. Model a bolt: half inch by four inches, hex head. Then place eighteen of them.
  2. Draw the frame on a TechDraw sheet, with a section through a tool bar.
  3. Design a third tyne — a spring tine that uses the same 5 inch head and 3 inch bolts.
  4. Sharpen the blade. Add a ground edge underneath the two cutting edges.
Summary and Exercises05 / 07

Exercises 9 to 12

Exercises: harder

Take it further

  1. Build a proper hitch. The machine as modelled has no top link point. Design an A-frame mast that ties into the brackets and the rear bar.
  2. One master parameter table. Move the bolt size and spacing into a single file that the frame and both tynes read from. Then change the bolt to 5/8 inch.
  3. Assemble it with joints instead of placements. Report anywhere the parts did not fit.

The big one

  1. Analyse a tyne. Take Tine 01 into the FEM workbench. Fix the head, push 150 pounds sideways at the point, and solve for stress. Is the section right, over-built, or marginal?

Exercise 12 is where this stops being drawing and starts being engineering. A model built the way this course built it — right material, right geometry, right weight — is ready for it.

Summary and Exercises06 / 07

Marking

How your work is judged

Five things, and the first one is not negotiable.

  • Every sketch says fully constrained.
  • Every dimension is in a spreadsheet, named, with its unit.
  • Assumptions are stated, with a reason.
  • The model rebuilds cleanly — nothing grey or failed anywhere in the tree.
  • Weight and overall size quoted, and checked against a rough hand calculation.

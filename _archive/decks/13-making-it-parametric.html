Making It Parametric01 / 06

The case for it

Why bother

It costs about twenty seconds a dimension. Here is what you get back.

  • You change, instead of rebuilding. Nine tynes become eleven in one cell.
  • Your reasoning is written down. “Inner rectangle = outer minus two member widths” says something. The number 78 does not.
  • Mistakes show up. Ask for fifteen tynes and the holes run off the end, on screen, not in the workshop.
  • Someone else can check it. Fifteen named cells can be reviewed. Forty numbers hidden in a feature tree cannot.

Three layers

Keep it in three layers

Separate the numbers you chose from the numbers that follow from them.

Never put arithmetic in a dimension box. If a dimension needs 82 minus twice 2, that belongs in a named cell where it can be seen and checked.

Making It Parametric03 / 06

Cells, aliases, expressions

How to actually do it

Three steps. Most people stop after the second.

  1. Type the name, the value with its unit, and what it means82 in
  2. Select the value cell and click Alias, then type a name
  3. In any dimension box, click the small blue circle and type the nameParams.FrameLength

Always include the unit. A cell holding 82 is just a number; a cell holding 82 in is a length. Attach a plain number to a length and FreeCAD will either refuse or quietly treat it as millimetres.

Making It Parametric04 / 06

A worked change

Watch a change cascade

Change the tyne count to eleven and the frame length to 101 inches. Then recompute and watch.

  • The outer rectangle widens, and the inner one follows because it is defined as the outer minus two member widths.
  • The tube bore widens with it.
  • The first hole moves to the new starting position.
  • The pattern spreads eleven stations across the new span.
  • The hitch brackets stay put, because their spacing is set by the tractor, not the frame.

Five changes from two cells, and not one of them needed a decision.

Where a parametric model breaks

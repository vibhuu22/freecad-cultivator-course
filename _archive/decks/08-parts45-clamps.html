Offset, or sweep?

Two ways to make a bent strap

How the tutorial does it

  1. Draw the bent centre line
  2. Round the corners
  3. Use Offset Entities to push a copy inward0.5 in
  4. Close the two ends to make a closed shape
  5. Extrude it3 in

How we do it

  1. Draw the bent centre line
  2. Round the corners
  3. Draw the strap's cross-section on a separate sketch
  4. Additive Pipe: push the section along the line

Same solid either way. But now the model knows the section and the path as two separate things you can edit. Change the section to 4 by 3/8 and the strap re-sweeps along the same path.

The tool worth learning here

Additive Pipe replaces two SolidWorks tools

Push a shape along a path. That is all a sweep is, and it covers a lot of ground.

  • It replaces Offset Entities for making a strap out of a line.
  • It replaces Weldments for making a beam out of a path — a structural member is a section swept along a line.
  • It is how we build the curved blades in the next deck.

The rounded corners come out of the sweep itself. There is no separate fillet feature to add or to break.

Sketching the bend

The path

Three straight runs from the origin. Dimension each one by how far it goes across and how far up.

  1. Draw three connected lines starting at the origin
  2. Make the first vertical and the last horizontal
  3. Dimension each run and rise, rather than a length and an angle
  4. Round the two cornersR2

When you round a corner, tick create corner. The fillet trims the lines back, so without it your 18 inch dimension quietly starts measuring the trimmed line instead of the real one.

Check after you fillet

The radius the fillet forgets

Rounding a corner in a sketch creates the arc but does not dimension it.

  • Before the fillets the sketch was fully constrained.
  • After two fillets it can move again — one loose end per corner.
  • Add a Radius dimension to each new arc and it is solid again.

This is the single most commonly forgotten step in the whole Sketcher. Look at the message in the Sketcher panel after every fillet.

Parts 4 and 5: The Clamps05 / 08

Making the sweep easy

The section, and where it sits

A sweep needs its section sitting on the start of the path, square to it.

  • Our path starts at the origin heading straight up.
  • So the section goes on the XY plane, centred on the origin. No offsets, no datum planes.
  • Then run Additive Pipe: pick the section, then pick the path.

Choosing a path that starts at the origin along an axis is not laziness. It is what makes the section sketch trivial. Design the path around that.

A stated assumption

What these parts are for

Honest answer: the tutorial never says.

  • It calls them “the attachment clamp” and gives dimensions, but never mates them to anything.
  • The 1 inch hole is the same size as the hitch pin, and a three-point mounted implement needs a top link anchor.
  • So we model the dimensions given and stand them on the rear bar as a mast.

If you disagree, the parts are still right. Only where they sit in Deck 10 changes. Joining the two braces into a proper A-frame is exercise 9.

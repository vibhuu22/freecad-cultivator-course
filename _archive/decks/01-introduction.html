Why this machine exists

What a cultivator does

It is a secondary tillage tool. The plough has already turned the soil. The cultivator breaks it down.

  • Breaks clods. The tyne cracks the furrow slice the plough left behind.
  • Kills weeds. The blade runs just under the surface and cuts weed roots.
  • Saves moisture. Loosening the top few inches slows water evaporating from below.

The tillage sequence

Where it fits in the field work

Every tillage tool has its place in the sequence. The cultivator is the second pass.

PassToolWorking depth
FirstMouldboard or disc plough8 to 12 inches
SecondCultivator — this machine4 to 9 inches
ThirdDisc harrow or tine harrow2 to 4 inches
LastRoller or plankersurface only

Nine tynes at 9.5 inch spacing cover 76 inches in one pass. That suits a 35 to 45 horsepower tractor.

A 5 + 4 arrangement

Why the tynes are in two rows

Look at the machine from above. The nine tynes sit on two bars, not one.

  • Five on the front bar, 19 inches apart.
  • Four on the rear bar, also 19 inches apart, but shifted 9.5 inches sideways.
  • Seen from the front the nine points are evenly spread, 9.5 inches apart.

If all nine sat in one row, straw and stubble would jam between them. Two rows give each tyne room to clear.

How we work

A model, not a drawing

Every dimension in this machine sits in a spreadsheet cell. Change the cell and the model rebuilds itself.

  • Want eleven tynes instead of nine? Change one number.
  • Want the blade bent on a bigger radius? Change one number.
  • The model works out the mass for you, so you can check the tractor can lift it.

This is what people mean by parametric. It takes a little more care while you build, and it saves hours later.

Reading the source

Where the design comes from

This course follows a SolidWorks tutorial by Malviya CAD Solution. The original is in Hindi, and we worked from a rough translation.

  • Some numbers in the translation are garbled. One line calls a hole “12.5”, another calls it “3 inches”.
  • Neither is a diameter. The 3 inches turned out to be the spacing between a pair of holes.
  • So we read the tutorial, check it against what the machine has to do, and then decide.

Wherever we had to decide something the source did not say, the slide says so. Deck 14 lists all twelve of those decisions in one place.
